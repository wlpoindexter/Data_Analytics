100

b) What happened to the line for Milkshake? What do the high and low look like now?

The Milkshake line changed scale and dropped slightly.
The highs and lows look lower because the data is now normalized against Hamburger.


5. Why did the line for Milkshake change? Hint: Click on the icon next to “Interest
over time” for more information about how trend results are displayed.

The line changed because Google Trends shows relative search interest, not absolute numbers.
When you add another term, all data is re-scaled from 0–100 based on the highest point among all terms.

6. Add a third term to your search, this time for Breakup (Topic).



a) Do you see any patterns that suggest a relationship between searches for Breakup
and Hamburger?

There is no strong or consistent pattern. The trends do not clearly move together.

b) How about between Breakup and Milkshake?

There is also no clear relationship. Peaks in Milkshake searches do not consistently align with Breakup searches.

7. What does all of this suggest about the reliability of the conclusions reached in the
article?

This suggests the conclusions are not very reliable. The article assumes that search interest equals behavior, but the data does not show a clear relationship between breakups and specific foods. The use of relative data and limited food options also weakens the conclusions.
